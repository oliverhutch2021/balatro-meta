How it plays

A faithful game, a fair player

The engine

Our own copy of Balatro 1.0.1o in Rust: all 150 jokers, 52 consumables, 32 vouchers, 24 tags and 28 boss blinds. The same seed deals the same cards, shops and packs as the real game, and the engine is checked move by move against the real game.

Fair play

The bot sees only what the screen shows. It never sees the seed, the draw order or the next shop. When it looks ahead, it plays on copies whose hidden cards are reshuffled, the way a player imagines what might come.

Deciding by value

Every move, from a shop purchase to the cards in a hand, is tried on copies and judged by one number: the chance of beating each blind ahead, the run's scoring power later, and money as buying power. A network trained on its own runs adds its estimate of the chance of winning.

How it learns

Play, label, learn, repeat

Each round, the best bot so far plays 25,000 new runs. Every position it saw is labelled with how its run ended, and a network learns the chance of winning from any position. The next round plays with that network.

PlayThe best bot plays 25,000 fresh seeds, about 4 million positions.
LabelEach position is marked won or lost by how its run ended.
LearnA network learns the chance of winning from the screen alone, on the GPU in minutes.
Play betterThe bot decides with the network's estimate. Round 4 gained 1.75 points, round 5 only 0.4.
ExploreNew from round 7: one decision in ten takes another option rated nearly as good, so the network sees where untried choices lead.

Without exploration, the network only learns from the choices the bot already makes, so each round can only refine its habits. That is why the gains were shrinking. Rounds 7 to 10 explored, round 10 with whole builds (a quarter of its runs commit from ante 3 to one hand picked at random), and their networks are no better than round 7's. The last step came from giving the networks more say against the formula: +2.6 points on 8,000 seeds.

Where it loses

Late, and just short

Of 25,000 runs in round 5's recording, 10,239 were lost, 82% of them at antes 5 to 8. A losing round typically reaches about three quarters of its target: the bot runs out of scoring as the targets climb.

The human teacher

A human player's lessons, tested

The project's human player wins about three runs in four at White Stake and records those runs move by move, so the bot's choice can be set against the player's at every decision. The player's losses come early (antes 1 to 3); the bot's come late. Each lesson below became an option and was tested on the same 2,000 seeds with and without it.

Focus on one hand, tested with fairer pack skipping on 8,000 seeds, gained 1.64 points (p = 0.001) and is now how the bot plays. None of the other gains is clear on 2,000 seeds, and the rules added as extra points (x mult, a thinner deck) made the bot worse: they skew everything else it weighs. The lessons that help will have to be found by the bot itself, which is what exploration is for. The player's runs show what it is looking for: on 1 November one took Straight from level 1 to 19 in one run and Flush to level 33 in another, where the bot's main hand ends its wins at a median level 5.
